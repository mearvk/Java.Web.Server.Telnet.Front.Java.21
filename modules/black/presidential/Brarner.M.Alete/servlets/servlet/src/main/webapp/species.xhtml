Animalia Classes

Warm-blooded and cold-blooded vertebrates, arthropods, and invertebrates across 5 major classes.

▶ Mammalia 153 families · 6,400 species · Jun 2026

Warm-blooded vertebrates characterized by mammary glands, hair/fur, and three middle ear bones.

▶Primates16 families · 500+ species

Includes apes, monkeys, lemurs, tarsiers. Highly developed brains and opposable thumbs.

▶HominidaeGreat Apes · 7 species
▶Homo sapiensHuman

Modern humans. ~8 billion global population. Tool use, language, culture.

▶Pan troglodytesChimpanzee

Closest living relative to humans. Complex social structures and tool use.

▶Gorilla gorillaWestern Gorilla

Largest living primates. Herbivorous, ground-dwelling. Critically endangered.

▶CercopithecidaeOld World Monkeys · 138 species
▶Macaca mulattaRhesus Macaque

Most widely used primate in biomedical research. Native to South/SE Asia.

▶Papio anubisOlive Baboon

Most widespread baboon species. Omnivorous, terrestrial. Complex hierarchies.

▶LemuridaeLemurs · 21 species
▶Lemur cattaRing-tailed Lemur

Endemic to Madagascar. Distinctive black-and-white ringed tail. Highly social.

▶Carnivora16 families · 286 species

Cats, dogs, bears, seals, weasels. Specialized carnassial teeth for shearing flesh.

▶FelidaeCats · 41 species
▶Panthera leoLion

Only social cat. Prides of 10–15. Males have manes. African savanna.

▶Panthera tigrisTiger

Largest living cat. Solitary, territorial. Endangered — ~4,500 in wild.

▶Acinonyx jubatusCheetah

Fastest land animal (~120 km/h). Non-retractable claws. African plains.

▶CanidaeDogs · 36 species
▶Canis lupusGray Wolf

Ancestor of domestic dogs. Pack hunters. Apex predator across Northern Hemisphere.

▶Vulpes vulpesRed Fox

Most widespread wild carnivore. Solitary, omnivorous, highly adaptable.

▶UrsidaeBears · 8 species
▶Ursus arctosBrown Bear

Includes grizzly subspecies. Omnivorous, hibernates. Up to 600 kg.

▶Ailuropoda melanoleucaGiant Panda

Bamboo specialist. Native to central China. Conservation success story.

▶Rodentia33 families · 2,277 species

Mice, rats, squirrels, beavers, porcupines. Continuously growing incisors.

▶MuridaeOld World Rats/Mice · 730 species
▶Mus musculusHouse Mouse

Most common lab mammal. Commensal with humans worldwide.

▶Rattus norvegicusBrown Rat

Global invasive species. Highly intelligent, social. Lab strain source.

▶SciuridaeSquirrels · 289 species
▶Sciurus carolinensisEastern Gray Squirrel

Common across eastern North America. Scatter-hoarder of nuts and acorns.

▶CastoridaeBeavers · 2 species
▶Castor canadensisNorth American Beaver

Ecosystem engineer — builds dams creating wetland habitats. Second-largest rodent.

▶Chiroptera21 families · 1,400+ species

Bats. Only mammals capable of sustained flight. Echolocation in most species.

▶PteropodidaeFruit Bats · 197 species
▶Pteropus vampyrusLarge Flying Fox

Wingspan up to 1.5m. Frugivorous. Key pollinator and seed disperser in SE Asia.

▶VespertilionidaeEvening Bats · 490 species
▶Myotis lucifugusLittle Brown Bat

Common in North America. Insectivorous. Threatened by white-nose syndrome.

▶Artiodactyla10 families · 550 species

Even-toed ungulates: deer, cattle, pigs, camels, hippos, whales.

▶CervidaeDeer · 55 species
▶Odocoileus virginianusWhite-tailed Deer

Most widespread deer in the Americas. ~30 million in US alone.

▶Alces alcesMoose

Largest deer species. Solitary. Broad palmate antlers in males.

▶BovidaeCattle/Antelope · 143 species
▶Bos taurusDomestic Cattle

~1 billion worldwide. Domesticated ~10,000 years ago. Ruminant digestion.

▶Bison bisonAmerican Bison

North American icon. Nearly extinct by 1890, recovered to ~500,000.

▶ Aves 248 families · 10,800 species · Jun 2026

Feathered, winged, bipedal endothermic vertebrates.

▶Passeriformes140 families · 6,500+ species

Perching birds — songbirds, corvids, finches. Largest avian order.

▶CorvidaeCrows/Ravens · 133 species
▶Corvus coraxCommon Raven

Largest passerine. Problem-solving intelligence. Widespread across Northern Hemisphere.

▶Cyanocitta cristataBlue Jay

Eastern North America. Vocal mimic. Caches acorns for winter.

▶TurdidaeThrushes · 175 species
▶Turdus migratoriusAmerican Robin

Iconic North American songbird. Orange breast. Early spring migrant.

▶Accipitriformes4 families · 266 species

Diurnal raptors — eagles, hawks, kites, vultures.

▶AccipitridaeHawks/Eagles · 249 species
▶Haliaeetus leucocephalusBald Eagle

US national bird. Fish eagle. Conservation success — removed from endangered list 2007.

▶Aquila chrysaetosGolden Eagle

Most widely distributed eagle. Hunts rabbits, marmots. 2m wingspan.

▶Psittaciformes4 families · 398 species

Parrots, macaws, cockatoos. Zygodactyl feet and curved bills.

▶PsittacidaeTrue Parrots · 350+ species
▶Ara macaoScarlet Macaw

Vibrant red/yellow/blue plumage. Central/South American rainforests. 50+ year lifespan.

▶Psittacus erithacusAfrican Grey Parrot

Exceptional vocal mimicry and cognitive ability. Can learn 1,000+ words.

▶Strigiformes2 families · 254 species

Owls. Nocturnal raptors with binocular vision and silent flight.

▶StrigidaeTypical Owls · 220 species
▶Bubo virginianusGreat Horned Owl

Most widespread owl in the Americas. Apex nocturnal predator. Ear tufts.

▶Strix variaBarred Owl

Eastern US forests. "Who cooks for you?" call. Expanding westward.

▶ Reptilia 92 families · 11,700 species · May 2026

Cold-blooded, air-breathing vertebrates with scales or scutes.

▶Squamata61 families · 10,900+ species

Lizards, snakes, amphisbaenians. Scaled reptiles with moveable quadrate bones.

▶ColubridaeColubrids · 1,900+ species
▶Pantherophis guttatusCorn Snake

Southeastern US. Popular pet snake. Docile, non-venomous constrictor.

▶Thamnophis sirtalisCommon Garter Snake

Most widespread snake in North America. Mildly venomous to prey, harmless to humans.

▶ViperidaeVipers · 360 species
▶Crotalus atroxWestern Diamondback

Most medically significant rattlesnake in US. Heat-sensing pit organs.

▶Testudines14 families · 360 species

Turtles, tortoises, terrapins. Bony or cartilaginous shell.

▶CheloniidaeSea Turtles · 6 species
▶Chelonia mydasGreen Sea Turtle

Tropical/subtropical oceans. Herbivorous as adults. Endangered. Nests on beaches.

▶Caretta carettaLoggerhead Sea Turtle

Large head, powerful jaws. Nests along NC Outer Banks. Vulnerable.

▶Crocodylia3 families · 27 species

Crocodiles, alligators, gharials. Semi-aquatic apex predators.

▶AlligatoridaeAlligators/Caimans · 8 species
▶Alligator mississippiensisAmerican Alligator

SE United States. Up to 4.5m. Conservation success — recovered from near-extinction.

▶CrocodylidaeTrue Crocodiles · 16 species
▶Crocodylus porosusSaltwater Crocodile

Largest living reptile. Up to 7m. Indo-Pacific. Most dangerous crocodilian to humans.

▶ Amphibia 74 families · 8,400 species · May 2026

Ectothermic tetrapods with aquatic larval stage.

▶Anura (Frogs)55 families · 7,300+ species

Tailless amphibians with long hind limbs adapted for jumping.

▶DendrobatidaePoison Dart Frogs · 200+ species
▶Phyllobates terribilisGolden Poison Frog

Most toxic vertebrate on Earth. One frog has enough poison to kill 10 humans. Colombia.

▶Dendrobates tinctoriusDyeing Dart Frog

Highly variable color morphs. Guiana Shield rainforests. Aposematic coloration.

▶HylidaeTree Frogs · 700+ species
▶Hyla cinereaGreen Tree Frog

Southeastern US. Bright green with white lateral stripe. Loud choruses at night.

▶Urodela (Salamanders)10 families · 760 species

Tailed amphibians with elongated bodies. Many capable of limb regeneration.

▶AmbystomatidaeMole Salamanders · 37 species
▶Ambystoma mexicanumAxolotl

Neotenic — retains larval features throughout life. Extraordinary regeneration ability. Critically endangered in wild (Lake Xochimilco).

▶Gymnophiona (Caecilians)10 families · 214 species

Limbless, serpentine amphibians. Mostly fossorial (burrowing).

▶CaeciliidaeCommon Caecilians · 42 species
▶Caecilia tentaculataLinné's Caecilian

South American. Tentacle organ between eye and nostril for chemoreception. Burrowing lifestyle.

▶ Insecta 660 families · 1,000,000+ species · Apr 2026

Six-legged arthropods with compound eyes and exoskeleton.

▶Coleoptera (Beetles)166 families · 400,000+ species

Hardened forewings (elytra). Most species-rich order on Earth.

▶CoccinellidaeLadybugs · 6,000 species
▶Coccinella septempunctataSeven-spot Ladybird

Major aphid predator. Biological pest control. Red with 7 black spots.

▶ScarabaeidaeScarab Beetles · 30,000+ species
▶Dynastes herculesHercules Beetle

Longest beetle (up to 17cm with horn). Can lift 850× body weight. Central/South America.

▶Lepidoptera (Moths/Butterflies)126 families · 180,000+ species

Scale-winged insects. Complete metamorphosis with caterpillar larval stage.

▶NymphalidaeBrush-footed Butterflies · 6,000 species
▶Danaus plexippusMonarch Butterfly

4,800km annual migration. Milkweed-dependent. Orange/black warning coloration.

▶Vanessa carduiPainted Lady

Most widespread butterfly on Earth. Found on every continent except Antarctica.

▶Hymenoptera (Ants/Bees/Wasps)132 families · 153,000+ species

Membrane-winged insects. Many eusocial with complex colony structures.

▶ApidaeBees · 5,700 species
▶Apis melliferaWestern Honey Bee

Primary managed pollinator. Colony of 60,000. Waggle dance communication.

▶Bombus terrestrisBuff-tailed Bumblebee

Key pollinator in Europe. Can buzz-pollinate (sonication). Colonies of 200–600.

▶FormicidaeAnts · 22,000 species
▶Solenopsis invictaRed Imported Fire Ant

Invasive worldwide from South America. Aggressive, venomous sting. Supercolonies.

▶Diptera (Flies)188 families · 152,000+ species

True flies with single pair of wings. Halteres for balance in flight.

▶DrosophilidaeFruit Flies · 4,000 species
▶Drosophila melanogasterCommon Fruit Fly

Premier genetic model organism. 75% of human disease genes have fly homologs. 10-day generation.

▶CulicidaeMosquitoes · 3,600 species
▶Aedes aegyptiYellow Fever Mosquito

Primary vector for dengue, Zika, chikungunya, yellow fever. Tropical/subtropical worldwide.

Plantae Classes

Land plants across 3 major classes — flowering, monocots, and conifers.

▶ Magnoliopsida 416 families · 300,000+ species

Flowering plants (dicots). Largest class of land plants covering trees, shrubs, herbs.

▶ Liliopsida 90 families · 70,000+ species

Monocotyledons including grasses, orchids, palms, and lilies.

▶ Pinopsida 7 families · 630 species

Conifers: pines, spruces, firs, cedars, cypresses.

Fungi Classes

Decomposers and symbionts across 2 primary classes.

▶ Agaricomycetes 120 families · 21,000+ species

Mushrooms, bracket fungi, puffballs. Primary decomposers in forest ecosystems.

▶ Sordariomycetes 90 families · 10,000+ species

Flask fungi including plant pathogens and endophytes.

Protista Classes

Unicellular eukaryotes across 2 phyla.

▶ Ciliophora 50 families · 4,500 species

Ciliated protozoans including Paramecium, Stentor, Vorticella.

▶ Apicomplexa 30 families · 6,000 species

Parasitic protists including Plasmodium (malaria) and Toxoplasma.
